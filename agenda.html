About this talk

Behavioral AI draws on attention, cognition, and emotion research to build technology that works with, rather than against, how people naturally perceive and act. Wearable research platforms like Project Aria, paired with multimodal sensing and machine learning, open new ways to study behavior egocentrically. I'll walk through recent case studies from our team: gaze dynamics and reading recognition in real-world settings, turn-taking prediction in conversation, and the hard problem of assessing cognition and emotion in the wild, along with what these mean for contextual AI and personalized computing.

About the panel

Bringing together perspectives from academic behavioral health research, clinical medicine, and industry research, this panel explores what it takes to move behavioral health XR beyond promising prototypes toward responsible real-world use. The discussion will consider how we define success, where translation from research to practice breaks down, and what responsibilities emerge as XR becomes increasingly intelligent, adaptive, and sensor-rich.
